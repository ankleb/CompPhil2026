Finding parallels

Compare two texts, find the passages they share, and sort them into one-to-one parallels (found once in each text), repeated matches (one place matches several places of the other text), and recurring phrases (formulae found many times in either text, listed once with all their occurrences). Click a highlighted passage, or a row of the table, to see both places side by side. Leave text B empty to find the passages repeated within text A. For two short texts, the simpler fuzzy matching page may be enough.

What to compare

Matching

Tokenize by

What can be found at all: the number of tokens in a row (syllables, or words when tokenizing by whitespace) that must agree exactly. A variant reading breaks such a run, so with many variants or OCR errors use a smaller size (4–5). Nearby agreeing runs are then joined into longer passages, across variants (see “Allowed gap”). Larger: fewer chance matches, but short or varied parallels are missed.

times

An ngram found more often than this in either text is treated as a formula (athāto … vyākhyāsyāmaḥ, iti ha smāha …): it is listed under “Recurring phrases” instead of being paired place by place. Raise it to pair more of the repeated material; lower it to set more aside as formulae.

An ngram found in more units than this still counts for the similarity, but is not used to decide which units to compare. Raise it if short units with common words find no partner.

tokens

How many tokens without a shared ngram (a variant reading, an added word or pāda) a passage may run through and still continue as one passage. Larger: long parallels with many variants stay together, but unrelated matches nearby may be joined to them.

tokens

What is listed: after joining, passages shorter than this are left out. It does not change what is found, only what is shown; raise it to hide short, trivial matches (with syllables, 12 is about a pāda). A value below the ngram size has no effect.

%

How much of a passage must be covered by shared ngrams. Low values keep loose parallels with many variants, but also chance matches joined across a gap; 100% means the passages agree throughout.

How alike two units must be to count as a pair: the share of ngrams they have in common (100% = the same ngrams). Lower it for parallels with more variants, raise it to keep only close ones.

References and markup

A regular expression; each match labels the text after it, until the next one.

By default: lines starting with %, commands like \susruta{…} or \esusruta, numbers like 1.1.3ab at a line start, and OCR page breaks.
